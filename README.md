# Pixel Studio

A design program that runs in the browser: Photoshop, Illustrator and InDesign in one —
photo editing, drawing, vector shapes, text and page layout.

The goal has two halves, and both matter equally:

- **Professional results.** Anything you can make with the professional tools, you should be
  able to make here, at the same quality. Advanced features are not left out; they are made
  easy to reach.
- **Simple enough for a 15-year-old** who has never used a design program. Every feature is
  explained in plain words, starts from a good default, and can be tried before it's kept.

## Design principles

Pixel Studio should feel obvious to a 15-year-old and still produce professional work.

- **Start from what you're making.** The first screen asks "What are you making today?" and
  offers ready-made sizes (square post, phone story, A4 page, poster…), and nothing else: the
  tools and panels only appear once there is a design to use them on. One click and you're in.
  People coming back see their own designs first, with a preview of each. A new, empty page shows
  what can go on it first (Add words, Add a photo, Add a shape, Draw), and starts on Arrange, so a
  first click never paints by surprise. Photo has its own button in the tool column.
- **Ask in your own words.** The "What do you want to do?" search (Ctrl+K) finds any action from
  everyday words: "save" finds Download, "bigger" finds Zoom in, "photo" finds Open. Whole
  sentences work too: little words like "make", "it" or "the" don't have to match, so "make it
  bigger" and "background color" find what was meant.
- **Everything explains itself.** Every tool, button, menu item and option has a short,
  plain-language tip on hover. Options show a live note that describes the current choice.
  Buttons that can't be used yet still explain why and what to do first.
- **The program always says what will happen.** A hint at the top of the canvas describes what
  the current tool does, and Undo names the exact step it will take back ("Undo brush stroke").
- **Drawing that looks good from the first stroke.** Ready-made brushes (Pencil, Ink pen, Marker,
  Soft brush, Highlighter) each show a live sample. "Steady hand" smooths shaky lines, pen
  pressure works on drawing tablets, and one stroke never gets darker where it crosses itself.
- **Everything is an object.** Pictures, photos and shapes are all things you click to pick, drag
  to move, pull a corner to resize and turn with the round handle, all with one tool (Arrange).
  Pictures keep their original pixels, so making something tiny and then big again never blurs
  it. Bring forward and send backward put things in front of or behind each other.
- **Lining up happens by itself.** While you drag something, it clicks into line with the page's
  middle and edges and with other things' edges and middles, and into even gaps between things;
  thin pink guides show what lined up, and gaps show their size. Pulling a side or corner and
  drawing a new shape line up the same way. Hold Alt to place it freely. A click or a tap never moves
  anything by accident.
- **Several things at once, without folders.** Drag a box around things (or Shift+click them) to
  pick several; they move, resize, turn, recolor and delete together. "Keep together" makes them
  one thing: a click on any part picks it all, a double-click picks just that part, and the Layers
  panel shows it as one row that opens to show its parts. "Tidy up" makes a neat row or column with
  even gaps, and "Line up" lines up edges or middles (one thing lines up with the page).
- **Any shape you can imagine.** The Pen draws your own shapes point by point: click for sharp
  corners, press and drag for smooth curves, click the first point to close. Afterwards drag a point
  to move it, drag a side to bend it, click a side to add a point, and double-click a point to make it
  sharp or smooth. "Edit points" does the same for any rectangle, oval, star or line, and open
  curves can end in an arrow head.
- **Turn a picture into shapes.** "Turn into shapes" (pro: Image Trace) makes a logo, a drawing, a
  signature or a sketch into sharp shapes that stay crisp at any size and can be recolored: Black &
  white for one ink color, or Colors for 2 to 16 colors, with a Detail slider and a live preview.
  Each color becomes one smooth shape (holes included), kept together over the picture, which stays
  hidden below so nothing is lost.
- **Colors that fade.** Any shape can be filled with a gradient: pick a ready-made one, then click
  its markers to recolor them, drag them to move them, click the bar to add a color, fade a color
  to see-through, or make it round instead of straight.
- **A page, not a pile of sheets.** A new design is an empty page of a color (or see-through).
  Nothing has to be set up before you start: painting makes its own drawing, and photos, shapes
  and text each arrive as their own thing. Click an empty spot (or Page in the Layers panel) and
  the Color panel colors the page; the fill bucket does it too.
- **No old-fashioned tricks to learn.** The same results as the professional tools, without their
  hard ideas: drop a photo onto a shape and the shape is filled with it (no clipping masks), and a
  layer's adjustments stay changeable any time (no adjustment layers).
- **Words on a curve.** The Bend slider arches text up like a rainbow or down like a smile, all
  the way round to a circle. Pick a text and a shape together and "Along the shape" runs the words
  along its edge (a ring of words around a round badge); slide them along it or put them on its
  other side. Hebrew and Yiddish read right to left on the curve too, and it stays changeable.
- **English, Hebrew and Yiddish, all first-class.** Type right on the design. Hebrew and Yiddish
  read right to left by themselves; numbers, English words and punctuation inside them land where
  a reader expects, even where lines wrap. What you see while typing is exactly what you get. 382
  free fonts come built in, 84 of them with Hebrew letters (among them the classic David, Frank
  Ruehl, Miriam, Keter and Stam, and Taamey and Ezra with every vowel and cantillation mark) and
  36 with every Yiddish letter and vowel point; fonts made only for English still show Hebrew in a
  matching Hebrew font, never as boxes. Fonts are found by what they're for (headlines, easy
  reading, Torah and seforim, invitations, kids) or by a word like "wedding", and pointing at one
  shows it on the page before you pick it. "Add your own font" takes a .ttf, .otf, .woff or .woff2
  file from your computer: it stays under "Your fonts" for next time, and goes inside your designs
  and SVG downloads, so they look right on any computer.
- **Lists.** The Text panel's List row puts dots, numbers or letters before each paragraph: א ב ג
  on the right for Hebrew and Yiddish, a b c on the left for English. They count by themselves, an
  empty line gets none, wrapped lines stay lined up beside them, and text that runs on into another
  box keeps counting.
- **Try first, then decide.** Cropping and adjusting colors show the result live. Nothing changes
  until you press Apply (or Enter); Esc puts it back. While adjusting, press and hold on the design
  to compare with how it was. Arranging shows the exact size and angle while you drag, and Undo
  names the step if you change your mind.
- **Looks before sliders.** Adjust starts with ready-made looks (Black & white, Old photo, Vivid,
  Warm, Cool, Soft, Dramatic, Dreamy), each previewed on your own picture. The sliders underneath
  (brightness, contrast, color strength, warmth, blur, sharpen) fine-tune any look.
- **Straighten in one click.** Straighten (pro: Auto Straighten) finds the horizon, walls and other
  long straight lines in a photo and shows it turned level, grown just enough that no empty corners
  show. A slider turns it a little more or less; Apply keeps it as one undo step. Plain code on the
  photo's own pixels, nothing downloaded; a photo filling the page keeps every original pixel.
- **Crops start on the main thing.** Picking a crop shape (Square, 4:5, 16:9) places the frame over
  the most important part of the design instead of the plain middle (pro: Content-Aware Crop), and a
  photo dropped into a shape starts on its main thing; "Center on the main thing" does it again.
- **Small photos that print sharp.** "Bigger and sharper" gives a picked photo two or four times
  as many pixels while it stays the same size on the design, so it prints crisp. It is plain math on
  the photo's own pixels (careful resampling, then gentle sharpening only along real edges, with no
  light or dark rims), never AI, so nothing is made up. Press and hold the preview to compare.
- **Words from a picture.** A scanned flyer or a photo of a sign becomes real, editable text: the
  words in English, Hebrew or Yiddish are found and put where they were, about the same size and
  color, kept together above the photo. The word reader comes from Pixel Studio's own site (never
  another one, so it works behind filters like NetFree), the first time it is used.
- **Mistakes are safe.** Painting never goes onto the page, a shape, text or a locked photo: it
  goes on a drawing above, so the eraser only rubs out your drawing. An opened photo starts
  locked. Undo goes back up to 100 steps, and a stroke that made a new drawing is one step.
- **Nothing is ever lost.** Every design is kept in the browser as you work, with all its layers,
  and "Saved" at the top says so. Closing the design, the tab or the whole browser is always
  safe: it waits under "Your designs" on the start screen. Download it as a Project to keep a
  copy or move it to another computer. If the browser can't keep designs (some private
  windows), the top says "Not saved", and closing with changes that aren't downloaded asks first.
- **Downloads in plain words.** Best quality (PNG), Small file (JPG), Page (PDF) or Project. A page
  is made for Screen (a small file to send) or Printing. For printing it comes out at the design's
  real size (300 pixels to the inch, so an A4 design is an A4 page) or any size you type, in inches
  with centimeters shown. Text and shapes are drawn again at full print detail, so they stay sharp
  however big it prints, and Hebrew and Yiddish come out exactly as on screen. Print shops get an
  extra edge for cutting (bleed) and cut lines, tucked under "For print shops". Text in the PDF can
  be searched and copied: the letters lie invisibly over the picture, right-to-left words included.
- **Pages.** A design can have many pages (a menu, a booklet). A small row of pages sits at the
  bottom: + adds one, ⋯ copies, moves or deletes, Page Up and Page Down go between them. All pages
  share one size, undo works across them, and a PDF download holds every page.
- **Margins and columns.** Pages › Margins and columns… draws faint lines for space around the edge
  (None, Small, Medium, Large, or any size) and up to six columns. Things click to them while being
  moved, the choice shows live before it's kept, and the lines are never printed or downloaded.
- **Text that runs on from box to box.** "Continue in another box" in the Text panel adds a box
  beside the text (on the left for Hebrew and Yiddish); what doesn't fit in one box continues in the
  next, like newspaper columns. Typing, sizes and fonts follow the whole story; removing a box puts
  its words back. Faint numbered outlines show the boxes, even empty ones.
- **Tables.** Text › Table adds rows and columns of words for a menu, price list or timetable,
  right to left when the app is in Hebrew or Yiddish. Click a cell and type; Tab goes to the next
  cell and Tab in the last one adds a row. Rows grow to fit their words. The Table panel adds and
  removes rows and columns, turns on a heading row and stripes, chooses the lines and the space in
  cells, and colors each part; the Text panel sets how the words look. Pull the sides to make the
  columns wider, or a corner to make it all bigger. Downloads keep the words as real words.
- **Same on every page.** Pages › Show on every page puts the picked thing (a logo, a heading) on
  every page; changing it on any page changes it on all of them, and new pages get it too. Pages ›
  Add page numbers puts a number at the bottom of every page that counts itself. The Layers panel
  marks these, and one click keeps a thing on its own page again.
- **Ready layouts.** Invitations, flyers and posters to start from, on the start screen and under
  Menu › Ready layouts…. Each is made only of shapes, words and colors (never pictures), so every part
  can be clicked, moved and retyped, and each can be tried in six color sets before it's kept.
- **The design's own colors and text styles.** Colors kept under "This design's colors" are one click
  away anywhere in the design, and "Change it everywhere…" changes a color in every place it's used, on
  every page, live as you pick. A text's look can be saved as a style ("Title", "Details"); one click
  gives another text that look, and updating the style changes every text that uses it. A ready
  layout comes with its colors and text styles already kept.
- **Colors from your photos.** The Color panel shows the five or six main colors of the photos on
  the design (or of the picked photo), found by themselves: small bright colors count, big plain
  skies count less. Click one to use it. "Match design" gives the shapes and words on the page those
  colors, words always in a color that reads on what is behind them, shown first with Apply and
  Cancel (pro: color theme, recolor artwork).
- **Words always readable.** When picked words are hard to read on what is behind them (measured on
  the real pixels, the way accessibility checks do), a quiet note in the Text panel offers fixes:
  white or dark words, a color from the photos, a soft shadow, or a soft box behind them kept
  together with the words. Pointing at a fix shows it first (pro: contrast check).
- **Tidy the whole page.** With one action, things that are almost lined up (edges or middles a few
  pixels apart, with each other or the page) or almost evenly spaced move the few pixels to line up
  exactly. Nothing moves far, things already lined up stay, and you see it before you keep it.
- **AI comes from our own site.** Magic select, Remove background and Blur the background use a
  small AI that ships with the site (`public/models`, refreshed by `node scripts/models/fetch.mjs`),
  engine included, so they work behind filters like NetFree that block other sites. Nothing is
  fetched from anywhere else; a browser test blocks every other site and checks the AI still works.
- **Blur the background.** With a photo picked, Arrange › Blur the background (pro: Lens Blur,
  depth of field, portrait mode): the AI finds the main thing, which stays sharp, and everything
  behind it goes soft with a gentle edge, like a photo from an expensive camera. A slider sets how
  much, shown live; Apply keeps it as one undo step. Only the user's own pixels are used.
- **Calm, light and clean.** Soft grays, white cards, one blue for "you can click this", and
  simple line icons with labels. No decorative pictures, logos or emojis.

## Running it

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 20 or newer.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Then open the address it prints (usually http://localhost:5173).

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run dev` | Starts the program locally and reloads on every change |
| `npm run build` | Checks the types and builds the finished site into `dist/` |
| `npm test` | Runs the unit tests (zoom math, colors, undo history, brushes, layers, search, project files, adjustments, boxes, text layout and reading order) |
| `npm run test:e2e` | Opens the program in a real browser, on a normal and a sharp (Retina) screen, and uses every feature |
| `npm run lint` | Checks the code for common mistakes |

Before the first `npm run test:e2e` on a new computer, run `npx playwright install chromium` once.

## Putting it online

The finished site is plain files (`npm run build` puts them in `dist/`), so any static host works.
The live site runs on Railway, which builds and publishes `main` on every push. Cloudflare Pages
or Netlify work too: build command `npm run build`, output folder `dist`. `public/_headers` tells the host which files
may be kept long and which must always be checked for updates; `.node-version` picks Node 22.

Once online, Chrome and Edge offer to install Pixel Studio as an app (also from Menu → Install as
an app). The installed app opens in its own window, works without internet (`pwa/service-worker.js`
keeps every file of the current version), and opens pictures and `.pixel` projects double-clicked
on the computer.

## How the code is organized

```
src/
  editor/
    stroke.ts    The brush engine: dabs, smoothing, pressure, and painting into a layer
    selection.ts Selections as shapes (rectangle, oval, freehand) that add, take away or invert
    objects.ts   What's on a layer: drawing shapes and pictures, finding what's under the pointer
    arrange.ts   Moving, resizing and turning objects without losing any quality
    table.ts     Tables: rows that fit their words, columns right to left, and the plain parts they're drawn from
    compose.ts   Putting all layers together at full detail, for downloads and previews
    transform.ts Boxes with handles: resizing, turning, flipping and the math behind them
    flood.ts     Finding the area the fill bucket fills
    adjust.ts    Brightness, contrast, color strength, warmth, blur and sharpen, and the ready-made looks
    adjustSession.ts  Which part changes, quick previews at screen size, and the full-detail result
    history.ts   Undo and redo, with memory limits and "changed since download" tracking
    project.ts   Project files: every layer and setting in one file, checked carefully when opened
    library.ts   "Your designs": designs kept in the browser (IndexedDB)
    layers.ts    Adding, removing, moving and changing layers, and keeping layers together
    organize.ts  Lining things up, even gaps and tidying several things at once
    snap.ts      Lining up by itself while dragging, and the guides that show it
    path.ts      Pen paths: curves, their bounds, adding, bending and smoothing points
    gradient.ts  Gradient fills: ready-made gradients, drawing them, adding and flipping colors
    brushes.ts   The ready-made brushes and erasers
    color.ts     Color codes and the color picker math
    straighten.ts  Finding how far a photo leans (Sobel edges and a Hough transform; straightenBox.ts turns it)
    saliency.ts  Finding the important part of a picture, for crops and photos in shapes (smartPhoto.ts uses both)
    ...          The document, zoom math, drawing to screen, opening and downloading files
  text/
    layout.ts    Lines, wrapping, lining up and reading direction (with bidi-js for the Unicode rules)
    textLayer.ts Text objects: measured with the real fonts, drawn, and kept fitted to their box
    wrapAround.ts, avoid.ts  Text in a box flowing around pictures and shapes (text wrap)
    textCurve.ts Bent text and text along a shape's edge: each letter set on the curve
    fonts.ts     The built-in fonts: loaded only when used, with Hebrew stand-ins for English-only fonts
    fontCatalog.ts  The font list, made by scripts/fonts/fetch.mjs
  components/    The screens: top bar, tool settings, canvas, color, layers, dialogs, search, tips
  hooks/         The editor state (document, layers, undo), automatic saving and element sizes
  actions.ts     Every action, described once in plain words; the menu, search and tips read it
  tools.ts       The tools, their explanations and hints
  presets.ts     The ready-made sizes
  App.tsx        Connects everything and handles keyboard shortcuts
e2e/             Browser tests that use the program like a person would
```

Under everything is the page: just a color, or nothing for a see-through design. On it are three
kinds of layers. Layers kept together share a group (a name and an id) and always sit next to each
other in the list, so there is no tree of folders: drawing, saving and undo treat them like any
other layers, and picking one picks its whole group. A picture layer (a drawing or a photo) is its own off-screen canvas. Moved by whole
pixels it just gets a new position; resized or turned it gets a box, and is drawn from its
original pixels into that box, so nothing is lost however often it changes. Only painting on it
redraws its pixels at the new size. It grows when you paint past its edge, so nothing is ever cut
off by accident. A shape layer and a text layer are each a description (kind, box, fill or gradient, outline, corners, pen points),
drawn fresh at any size; a photo inside a shape is kept whole, with how big it is and which part
shows. Cropping and resizing the design only move layers and change their boxes, so undo, or
resizing back, brings back everything.

A brush stroke paints onto a separate stroke canvas and is shown on a preview of the layer while
you draw; when you let go, the changed area is copied into the layer and kept for undo.

Text is typed into a real browser text box laid exactly over the design (same font, size, spacing,
turn and zoom), so the cursor, selecting, copying and every keyboard, including Hebrew and Yiddish,
work as they do everywhere. When typing ends, the text is drawn on the design with the same layout:
the reading direction of every letter is worked out for the whole paragraph by the Unicode rules
for mixing directions, the paragraph is cut into lines, and each line is drawn as pieces that each
read one way. The baseline is measured from the browser itself, so nothing moves by even a pixel.
A browser test types mixed Hebrew, Yiddish, English, numbers and punctuation and checks that the
finished text matches what was on screen while typing.

The fonts are free fonts from Google Fonts (through Fontsource), under the SIL Open Font License or
the Apache License, the Culmus Hebrew fonts and the Taamey fonts, under the GNU GPL with the font
exception (a design that uses them is not covered by the GPL), and Ezra SIL, under the SIL Open
Font License; each one's license is next to it in `public/fonts/<font>/LICENSE`. They're loaded
only when a design uses them and kept for offline use after that. `node scripts/fonts/fetch.mjs`
downloads them again and rebuilds the font list from `scripts/fonts/list.json`;
`python3 scripts/fonts/culmus.py` remakes the Culmus, Taamey and Ezra files first. What each font
is good for is chosen by hand in `src/text/fontPurposes.ts`.

Words from a picture uses Tesseract through tesseract.js (Apache License 2.0). Its worker, engine
and English, Hebrew and Yiddish models are in `public/ocr/<version>/`, served by our own site and
kept by the browser after the first use; `node scripts/ocr/fetch.mjs` copies them again.

A project (`.pixel`) is the letters `PXSTUDIO`, a JSON description of the design (size, layers
with their kind, names, positions, boxes, visibility, opacity, locks and adjustments, shapes, text
with its font and settings, which layers are kept together, the page color, the selection), and then every picture's pixels as a PNG: each picture
layer, and each photo inside a shape, always at full size. Older projects (versions 1 to 4) still
open; those from before version 4 open with their paper as a layer. Designs kept in the browser use the same format. Saving happens a moment after
each change (and right away when you switch tabs or leave a design); only layers whose pixels
changed are encoded again, so saving stays quick on poster-size designs.

A whole layer keeps its adjustments as a setting: its pixels stay as they were, and the adjustment
can be changed or removed any time (the Layers panel shows which layers are adjusted). Adjusting
just a selected part changes those pixels.

Adjustments are worked out on the pixels themselves (no graphics-card tricks), so they look the
same in every browser. Previews only use as much detail as the screen shows. Blur is done on a
smaller copy when it's strong (a blurred picture has no fine detail to lose), and sharpening
compares each pixel with a soft copy the canvas makes by shrinking and enlarging the picture, which
keeps a 12-megapixel photo to well under a second. Blur repeats a photo's edge pixels rather than
pulling in see-through pixels, so blurred photos keep solid edges.

## Roadmap

1. **Foundation** (done) — start from a ready-made or custom size, open a picture (button or
   drag and drop), zoom and move around, download as PNG or JPG, search, hover help everywhere.
2. **Drawing** (done) — brushes and erasers with live samples, steady hand, pen pressure,
   straight lines, colors with a picker, swatches and recent colors, undo and redo, layers
   (add, duplicate, delete, reorder, rename, hide, opacity), protection against losing work.
3. **Selections, changes and saving** (done) — select areas (rectangle, oval, freehand; add,
   take away, invert), move, copy and paste, resize, turn and flip with handles, crop with
   ready-made shapes, resize or turn the whole design, fill bucket, automatic saving in the
   browser with "Your designs" on the start screen, project files with every layer, renaming.
4. **Adjustments and looks** (done) — ready-made looks with previews, brightness, contrast, color
   strength, warmth, blur and sharpen, on a layer or just the selected part, with press-and-hold
   to compare.
5. **Objects** (done) — everything on the design is an object: one Arrange tool picks, moves,
   resizes, turns and flips anything without losing quality; shapes that stay sharp and editable
   (rectangles with round corners, ovals, polygons, stars, lines and arrows) with fill and outline
   colors; add photos (menu or drag and drop); drop a photo onto a shape to fill it, then zoom and
   slide it inside; lock and delete objects; adjustments that stay changeable; a page with its own
   color (or see-through) instead of a background layer; painting that makes its own drawing; bring
   forward, send backward, to front and to back; lining up and even gaps by themselves while dragging;
   picking several things (box or Shift+click), keeping them together, tidy up and line up.
6. **Text** (done) — type on the design in English, Hebrew and Yiddish, with right-to-left text
   that never scrambles; quick headings, subheadings and paragraphs; 382 free fonts with live
   previews, found by what they're for, with Hebrew and Yiddish filters; size, boldness, italic, lining up, reading direction,
   letter and line spacing; boxes that wrap their lines; text resizes, turns and saves like any
   object.
7. **Shapes and the pen** (Illustrator) — a pen for curves with editable points (done); gradient
   fills (done); outline dashes and ends; combine shapes (join, cut out, overlap); SVG export.
8. **More text** — different fonts and colors inside one text, text along a path (done), text effects
   (outline, shadow, fill with a photo), your own font files (done), lists (done) and columns.
9. **Pages and print** (InDesign) — multi-page documents (done), margins and columns (done),
   text that flows from box to box (done), text that flows around pictures and shapes, close
   around their outline, around their box, or above and below them (done), tables for menus,
   price lists and timetables, right to left too (done), things and page
   numbers on every page (done), print-ready PDF (several pages, bleed, cut lines and
   selectable text are done).
10. **Pro layers and photo tools** (Photoshop) — blend modes, effects (shadow, glow,
   outline), curves and levels, clone and heal, select by color and select subject, and an easy
   way to fade or hide parts of a layer (with a brush, never a mask to learn).
11. **Ready to sell** — accounts, saving to the cloud, payments and a free trial.
